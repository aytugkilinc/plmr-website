Example 01 · Illustrative

Technical review: a terrace roof with side screens

The question: what must be clarified before a roof and screen configuration can be selected?

Starting information: a client sketch, approximate opening dimensions, site photos and a supplier brochure. None of these inputs is treated as a verified site survey or project-specific performance assessment.

A useful findings note separates issues from assumptions.

  • Support and fixings: identify the proposed attachment locations; ask the responsible professional to confirm substrate and fixing capacity.
  • Drainage: request the system's gutter and outlet details; record where the water is intended to go after discharge.
  • Movement and interfaces: identify roof and screen travel paths; request the supplier details needed to check clearances.
  • Performance: list the project requirements and seek evidence applicable to the selected size and configuration.
  • Decision status: record what can be compared now, what remains open and who must provide the missing information.

Possible handover: a written review, annotations to supplied material where agreed, an issue register and next actions. It is not a structural calculation, approval or release to manufacture.

Example 02 · Illustrative

Drawing coordination: making the handover explicit

The question: which documents help the project team coordinate roof, glazing, screens and drainage?

Possible agreed set: a drawing register, overall layout, relevant elevations or sections, interface details and an open-issues record. Each item identifies its revision and intended use.

What the handover should make clear

  • Units, drawing references and the origin of dimensions.
  • Verified information, supplied assumptions and unresolved inputs.
  • Which supplier details have been incorporated and their revisions.
  • Who confirms support capacity, performance, local requirements and release status.
  • What changed in the revision and what decision is still required.

Possible handover: the agreed drawings and a coordination record in the formats set out in the scope. This outline is not a technical drawing and does not establish that any project is ready for fabrication or installation.

Example 04 · Illustrative, not a completed client project

Custom detail: a roof rail meeting a different gutter.

Requirement: coordinate the rail-to-gutter connection where standard interfaces do not match.

Technical difficulty: mating profiles, fastening access, movement and the drainage route must be considered together.

PLMR’s contribution: review supplied geometry, develop an adapter or connection detail and prepare questions for the proposed fabricator.

Possible output: a component drawing, material/finish and tolerance requirements, a revision record and sample-review questions.

Still to verify: dimensions, applicable loads, material and fastener suitability, manufacturing capability and the agreed acceptance/release responsibilities. This scenario is explanatory and is not a fabrication or construction document.

Example 05 · Illustrative, not an actual inspection record

Order follow-up: make the outstanding items visible.

Requirement: support a buyer working with an existing Turkish supplier before dispatch.

Starting information: the agreed order, current drawings, scope and accessory list, supplier progress information and available packing documents.

Possible work: follow up drawing revisions and agreed order items; compare supplied packing information with the order; record the source, date and status of missing information. An agreed visit can add observations and permitted photographs.

Possible handover: a progress or visit note, document/packing checklist and outstanding-item register identifying who must respond and the next action.

The record distinguishes documents reviewed from items physically observed. Sampling, quality acceptance, transport and warranty responsibilities are agreed separately; this example is not an accredited inspection or shipment guarantee.
