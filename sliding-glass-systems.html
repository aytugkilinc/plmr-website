System overview

Plan where the panels travel and collect

Sliding glass walls move panels horizontally along tracks to open a patio or terrace enclosure. Framed and frameless systems have different component and glazing arrangements. Terms such as multi-slide patio doors should be used only where the selected system has that configuration.

Where it can fit

  • Pergola side enclosures
  • Terraces
  • Winter gardens
  • Verandas
  • Restaurant and café areas
  • Balcony and outdoor openings

Project decisions

Define the technical questions early

Track configuration

How many rails are needed for the required opening and panel stack?

Panel parking

Where do panels collect when open?

Threshold

Is a low threshold required and how is drainage handled?

Corners

How are L- or U-shaped openings treated?

Glass / insulation

What glass configuration is available in the selected system?

Interface

How does the track connect to floor, roof beam or pergola frame?

Technical checklist

Inputs to clarify before selection

  • Opening width

FAQ

Sliding glass or guillotine glass?

Sliding panels move horizontally and need track/parking space. Guillotine systems move vertically and use motorized panel movement. The correct route depends on space, use and technical constraints.

Does sliding glass make a space fully weather-tight?

Performance varies by system. Do not assume façade-level air, water or thermal performance unless the selected product documentation supports that requirement.

Can sliding glass be combined with a zip screen?

It may be possible, but track positions, frame depth, clearances and operating sequence need coordinated detailing.
