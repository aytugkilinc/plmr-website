How Drainage Works in a Bioclimatic Pergola Project

A useful drainage review follows the whole water path from roof surface to gutter, outlet, column or downpipe and final discharge.

PLMR Technical InsightsUpdated 10 Sep 2026

1. Roof collection

In many louvered-roof systems, water falling on closed blades is directed toward integrated gutters. The exact path depends on blade geometry, orientation, roof layout and selected product.

2. Perimeter gutters

The gutter must receive water and move it toward the intended outlet. Review location, fall direction, connected modules, debris zones and maintenance access.

3. Outlets

Outlet location should be coordinated before columns and surrounding construction are finalized. Confirm which column or dedicated outlet is used and what happens if that position conflicts with site conditions.

4. Columns and downpipes

Some systems route water through columns. A clean appearance does not eliminate the need for a real base discharge, connection to drainage and coordination with foundation/fixing.

5. Final discharge point

The supplier drawing may show water leaving the column; the site still needs to define what happens next. Coordinate floor falls, drains, waterproofing, thresholds, landscaping and underground drainage where applicable.

6. Side enclosures

Sliding glass, guillotine glass and zip screens change perimeter conditions. Water at the floor or lower track can affect thresholds, seals and internal finishes.

7. Performance and maintenance

Do not use a generic drainage-capacity number for all systems. Use verified data for the exact selected product and keep gutters/outlets accessible for maintenance.
