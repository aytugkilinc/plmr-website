How to Compare Supplier Quotations for Outdoor Systems

Two quotations can describe the same type of pergola, glazing system or screen while covering different products, accessories and responsibilities. A useful comparison starts by establishing what each supplier is actually offering.

PLMR Technical InsightsUpdated 15 Sep 2026

The aim is to identify whether the offers answer the same requirement, what information is missing and which questions must be resolved before a purchasing decision.

Start with one shared requirement

Give each supplier the same starting information: the intended use, project location, dimensions and units, required opening behavior, interfaces and available drawings.

Mark approximate or unverified information clearly. If an input changes, record the change and ask whether the quotation needs to be revised. Comparing offers based on different drawing revisions can hide differences that matter later.

For the starting information, use the pergola quotation preparation checklist.

Identify the proposed configuration

Record the product reference and the configuration to which the offer applies. Check which roof, glazing, screen, motor, controls, finishes and accessories are included.

A general brochure helps describe a product family. The quotation still needs to identify what is being offered for your requirement.

Separate included, excluded and unconfirmed items

Use those three labels consistently.

An item omitted from a quotation remains unconfirmed until the supplier clarifies it. Do not assume that a connection, accessory, drawing, transport service or installation task is included simply because the project will need it.

Keep the supplier's clarification with the quotation so the comparison can be traced back to written information.

Record the supporting documents

List the documents supplied, including their dates and revisions. Identify which project questions they answer and which remain open.

Where performance evidence or project-specific confirmation is required, ask for information applicable to the proposed configuration. Record who is responsible for reviewing it. This comparison is an information record; it does not itself verify structural adequacy or provide regulatory approval.

Make delivery and project responsibilities explicit

Identify who provides packing, freight, unloading, customs arrangements, site measurement, installation and after-sales support, where relevant.

For each responsibility, record the named party and the written scope. Keep supplier statements attributed to the supplier. Do not turn an indicative statement into a confirmed commitment.

Compare commercial terms after clarifying scope

Record the quoted currency, validity, payment conditions, exclusions and any conditions attached to the stated lead time.

Keep the original quoted amounts separate from your own assumptions or adjustments. If information is missing, flag the comparison as incomplete instead of filling the gap with an unsupported estimate.

Finish with a decision and clarification list

A useful comparison should leave you with:

  • The requirement against which the offers were reviewed.
  • The scope and source documents for each option.
  • Material differences between the offers.
  • Unanswered questions and the party expected to answer them.
  • The checks still needed before selection or order.
