Guillotine Glass vs Sliding Glass: How to Choose for an Outdoor Project

Both systems can close an outdoor opening, but their movement logic changes the architecture, controls, panel parking, threshold and clear opening.

PLMR Technical InsightsUpdated 10 Sep 2026

Guillotine glass

Guillotine glazing uses vertically moving glass panels, commonly with motorized operation. Panel collection and boundary behavior vary by manufacturer.

Sliding glass

Sliding systems move panels horizontally along tracks. The opening depends on rail count, panel width, parking direction and corner geometry.

1. Horizontal space vs vertical movement

Guillotine systems do not need horizontal panel parking in the same way as sliding glass. If the architecture has limited side parking space, this can materially affect the decision.

2. Motorized vs manual use

Guillotine systems are commonly selected for motorized vertical movement. Sliding glass may be manually operated depending on the selected system. Consider frequency of use, controls, service access and power supply.

3. Threshold and floor interface

Sliding systems use floor tracks that affect drainage, accessibility and cleaning. Guillotine systems use a different lower interface but still need a clear sill/base and drainage strategy.

4. Safety and guarding

If glazing forms a barrier at an elevated opening, applicable safety/guarding requirements must be checked. Do not assume the category automatically satisfies local rules.

5. Pergola integration

Coordinate column width, beam depth, head fixing, floor track/base, zip screens, door openings, electrical routes and rainwater paths before the pergola geometry is locked.
