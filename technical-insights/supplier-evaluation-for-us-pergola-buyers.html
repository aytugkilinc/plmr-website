Supplier evaluation

Evaluate a Pergola Supplier Against the Order You Plan to Place

A technical supplier evaluation starts with the proposed system, manufacturing route and evidence required for your actual order.

Written by Aytuğ Kılınç, Civil Engineer11 October 2026

Describe what the supplier must be able to deliver

Before requesting a shortlist, define the product family, configuration, quantities and destination. Include the drawing work, finish, accessories and documentation expected with the order. A supplier that makes the general product type still needs to confirm the proposed scope.

For a commercial patio project, describe the roof movement and adjoining glass or screen systems. For a profile or component order, provide the drawings, material requirement, tolerances and finish references available for review.

Request information that can be traced to the proposal

Ask who manufactures the system and which party will contract for the order. Request the current product details, proposed component schedule and written answers to the technical enquiry. The comparison should identify the document and revision supporting each entry.

Project examples can help explain a supplier’s product range, but they do not verify your own configuration. Ask the supplier to distinguish an existing product arrangement from any proposed modification or new detail.

Keep unanswered questions visible

A useful supplier comparison has space for unconfirmed information. Record whether a component is included, a drawing is preliminary or a requested report has not been supplied. Avoid filling a gap with an assumption from another product brochure.

Compare quotation scope before treating totals as equivalent. Differences in control equipment, accessories, packing and delivery responsibilities can leave the proposals covering different work.

Agree how the order will be followed up

Identify the current drawings, approved changes, production stage and target dispatch date. Establish who supplies progress information and how open items are resolved. If a local visit is useful, agree access, sampling and the reporting brief with the supplier in advance.

A visit record should distinguish items observed from information supplied by the manufacturer. PLMR’s supplier-check service describes the possible report and the scope that needs to be agreed.

Use a defined assignment rather than a general endorsement

PLMR can research supplier options or work with a Turkish manufacturer you already use. The assignment can produce an enquiry brief, source-linked comparison, clarification log or order follow-up record.

The recommendation is tied to the information reviewed and the agreed criteria. Review an illustrative comparison format and discuss the evidence your procurement team needs.
